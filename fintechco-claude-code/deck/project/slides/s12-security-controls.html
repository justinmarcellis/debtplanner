CTO, this is your section, and you have already seen most of it live, so I will be brief and precise. Five questions, five controls. What can it touch? Allow, ask and deny rules, evaluated deny first [CLM-606], and an operating-system sandbox, optional until your policy requires it, that limits what shell commands can write and which network domains they reach [CLM-607]; in the demo it ran tests but could not read the secrets file. What must it never do? Hooks enforce policy in code, like the card-number guard that blocked a write in Act one [CLM-611]. Who sets policy? Managed settings, a policy your administrators deploy centrally; nothing in an engineer’s own settings overrides it [CLM-675]. What does it record? Usage and events, including every permission decision, export through OpenTelemetry, an open telemetry standard, to your own collector and on to your SIEM, your security log system, with prompt text redacted by default [CLM-612] [CLM-684]. Where does it run? Through Amazon Bedrock or Google Cloud, with your own credentials, identity rules and billing [CLM-686] [CLM-687]. Data handling, retention and certifications are in the appendix and the security brief; I would expect your security team to test all of it.
